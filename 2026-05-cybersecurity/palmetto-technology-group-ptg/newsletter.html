Why SMBs Are Cybercriminals' #1 Target in 2026 — And What to Do Now

If you think your business is too small to be on a hacker's radar, the data says otherwise — and the stakes have never been higher.

For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken inflation and economic pressure as the top concern for small and mid-sized businesses, according to the VikingCloud 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report. That shift reflects a threat environment that has fundamentally changed. AI-powered tools now allow attackers to scan thousands of networks simultaneously, craft hyper-convincing phishing emails, and launch coordinated campaigns at scale — all targeting businesses like yours.

The numbers are stark. Small businesses with fewer than 100 employees receive 350% more threats than larger companies. Phishing and credential theft remain the most common entry points, and ransomware demands now average $2.73 million per incident. Perhaps most alarming: 40% of SMBs say a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less would shut them down for good. Meanwhile, 84% of business owners are still managing cybersecurity on their own — often without sufficient training or tools to keep pace with modern threats.

So what should your team actually do this month? CISA's Cyber Essentials guidance points to three high-impact starting points: enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all users, ensure automated backups of critical data, and deploy patches quickly across every system. Layer in regular employee phishing-awareness training, and you've addressed the most common attack vectors in one focused push.

These aren't complex, enterprise-only solutions — they're practical steps any SMB can take right now. The good news is you don't have to figure it out alone.
