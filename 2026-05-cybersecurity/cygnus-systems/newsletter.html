Why Cybercriminals Are Targeting Your Business More Than Ever in 2026

Cyber threats aren't just growing — they're accelerating. And if you run a small or mid-sized business, you're squarely in the crosshairs. Understanding what's changed and what to do about it could be the most important thing you do this month.

For the first time, cybersecurity has overtaken economic pressure as the top concern for SMBs in 2026, according to VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report. That shift reflects a new reality: attackers are no longer targeting large enterprises exclusively. AI-powered tools now let criminals automate attacks at scale, making it faster and cheaper to hit hundreds of smaller businesses at once. The result? SMBs now face 43% of all cyberattacks — despite representing just 30% of the business landscape.

The threat mix your team faces in 2026 looks familiar but hits harder. Phishing emails are now AI-crafted to bypass spam filters and impersonate trusted vendors or executives. Ransomware has evolved into "double extortion" — attackers steal your data first, then encrypt it, threatening public exposure unless you pay. According to the SonicWall 2026 Cyber Protect Report, 88% of SMB breaches involved ransomware, and the average cost of a single breach now exceeds $4.9 million when downtime and recovery are factored in. Alarmingly, the average breach goes undetected for 181 days. Meanwhile, CISA's guidance for small businesses continues to emphasize that most successful attacks exploit predictable, preventable gaps — not sophisticated zero-day exploits.

The good news: the defenses that matter most aren't out of reach. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all systems, endpoint detection and response (EDR), tested data backups, regular employee training, and a written incident response plan are the fundamentals that separate resilient businesses from vulnerable ones. Cyber Defense Magazine notes that deploying MFA broadly and building a clear recovery plan are the highest-impact steps SMBs can take right now.

Your IT environment shouldn't feel like a liability. If you're unsure where your biggest gaps are, a fresh assessment is the right place to start — and we're here to help you do exactly that.

TRENDING IN IT

FBI: Cybercrime Losses Hit $20.8B — BEC & AI Phishing Surge

The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report — released April 2026 — marks the first year cybercrime losses surpassed $20 billion, a 26% jump from 2024. Business email compromise topped $3 billion in losses, while AI-assisted phishing complaints nearly tripled in financial impact.

Healthcare and manufacturing — two sectors with significant SMB exposure — ranked among the hardest-hit industries. These aren't enterprise-only threats; SMBs without layered defenses remain easy targets.

CYBER THREAT UPDATE

Akira Ransomware Continues Targeting Small and Mid-Sized Businesses

CISA, the FBI, and HHS updated their joint advisory on Akira ransomware, warning that the group "primarily targets small and medium-sized businesses" across manufacturing, healthcare, financial services, and IT sectors.

Akira gains entry through unpatched VPN devices and stolen credentials — making patching systems and enabling multi-factor authentication two of the most effective steps businesses can take right now.
