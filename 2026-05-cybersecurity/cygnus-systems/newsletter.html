The Cyber Threats Targeting Your SMB Right Now — and What to Do

If you think cybercriminals are focused on big corporations, think again. In 2026, the threat has come directly to your door — and the numbers are hard to ignore.

According to recent research, small and mid-sized businesses now account for 43% of all cyberattacks — despite representing just 30% of the business landscape. What's driving that imbalance? Attackers have automated their playbooks. AI-powered tools let criminals launch sophisticated, personalized campaigns at scale, making every SMB a viable target — regardless of size or industry.

This year's threat landscape is defined by three compounding trends. First, AI-enhanced phishing: your employees are now receiving hyper-personalized emails, texts, and even voice calls crafted to impersonate vendors, managers, or executives. Second, ransomware continues to evolve — attackers now steal your data *before* encrypting it, threatening public exposure in what's known as double extortion. Third, the financial stakes keep rising. A VikingCloud survey found that 40% of SMBs say a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less could put them out of business — and for the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken economic pressure as SMBs' #1 concern.

The good news? A few foundational steps make a measurable difference. CISA's Cyber Essentials guidance for small businesses recommends starting with multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all accounts, regular tested data backups, employee phishing training, and a written incident response plan. Research confirms that employees who receive consistent simulation-based training are 7x less likely to fall for a phishing attempt.

For Southeast Michigan businesses — from Southfield law firms to Detroit-area manufacturers — these aren't abstract statistics. They're your clients' data, your team's productivity, and your reputation on the line. Layered, proactive security doesn't have to be overwhelming or expensive when you have the right partner.
