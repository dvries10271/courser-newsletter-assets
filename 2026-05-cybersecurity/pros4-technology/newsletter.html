Is Your Business Ready for 2026's Cyber Threats?

Cybercriminals aren't just targeting big corporations anymore — and the numbers in 2026 prove it. If you run a small or mid-sized business, the threat landscape has shifted squarely in your direction, and staying informed is the first step toward staying protected.

For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken economic concerns like inflation as the top worry for SMB owners, according to VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report. That's not just a headline — it reflects a genuine shift in how attacks are evolving. AI-powered tools now allow bad actors to scan thousands of networks simultaneously, making phishing campaigns more convincing and ransomware incidents more frequent. In fact, ransomware attacks on small businesses have increased 78% since 2024, and AI-assisted phishing has surged dramatically in sophistication.

The financial stakes couldn't be higher. For 40% of SMBs, a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less would force them to close permanently. And yet, 84% of business owners are still self-managing their cybersecurity — often without adequate training or tools. Meanwhile, CISA's guidance for small businesses is clear: the most impactful steps you can take right now include enabling multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all accounts, maintaining automated data backups, patching systems promptly, and training your staff to recognize phishing attempts. CISA also recommends having a written Incident Response Plan before you ever need it — because there's no time to build one in the middle of a crisis.

For businesses in Eastern Wisconsin, these aren't abstract concerns. Whether you're running a professional services firm, a municipality, or a multi-location operation, your data and your customers deserve layered, proactive protection — not a patchwork of consumer-grade tools.
