Why Cybercriminals Love Targeting SMBs — And What to Do Now

Cybercriminals aren't just going after the big fish anymore. If you run a small or mid-sized business in 2026, you're squarely in their crosshairs — and the stakes have never been higher.

For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken economic concerns like inflation as the #1 threat facing SMBs, according to VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report. That's a remarkable shift — and a wake-up call. Attackers have realized that smaller businesses often combine valuable data with weaker defenses, making them efficient targets. Smaller companies with fewer than 100 employees now receive 350% more threats than larger enterprises, yet many still rely on minimal or outdated protections.

The numbers paint a sobering picture. According to recent research, 40% of SMBs say a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less could shut them down for good. Meanwhile, AI-driven attacks are accelerating the threat: phishing campaigns have surged dramatically in sophistication, and criminals now use automation to scan thousands of business networks simultaneously. Ransomware remains a serious concern, with average demands reaching into the millions — and even businesses that pay often recover only part of their data.

So what can you actually do this month? CISA's Cyber Essentials guidance recommends starting with three immediate actions: enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) across all user accounts, implement automated and continuous data backups, and apply software patches and updates as quickly as possible. Beyond the technical basics, building a security-aware culture on your team matters just as much — phishing and credential theft remain the most common entry points for breaches, exploiting human behavior rather than system vulnerabilities.

FBI: Cybercrime Losses Hit $20.9B — SMBs Are in the Crosshairs

The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report logged over one million complaints and nearly $21 billion in losses — a 26% surge from 2024 — with business email compromise alone accounting for $3 billion. Phishing losses tripled year-over-year, driven by AI-powered attacks that are more targeted than ever.

CISA Warns of LummaC2 Credential-Stealing Malware

CISA and the FBI issued a joint advisory about LummaC2, an infostealer malware that quietly lifts login credentials, financial data, and even multi-factor authentication codes from infected computers. It typically arrives via phishing emails or fake software downloads, and often runs without leaving detectable traces.
