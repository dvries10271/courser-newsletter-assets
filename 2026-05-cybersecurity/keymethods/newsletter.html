Why SMBs Are Cybercriminals' #1 Target in 2026

Cyberattacks have officially surpassed inflation as the number one concern for small and mid-sized businesses in 2026. If that surprises you, the data behind it shouldn't — and knowing what's driving that shift is the first step to protecting your operations.

The threat landscape has changed dramatically. Criminals no longer handpick targets — automated tools scan the internet around the clock looking for unpatched software, weak passwords, and open vulnerabilities. When they find one, the attack launches. According to the Verizon 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report, ransomware appeared in 88% of SMB breaches last year — more than double the rate seen at large enterprises. And ransomware attacks overall jumped 34% in 2025, with U.S. incidents up 50% in just the first ten months of the year.

AI is making things worse. LLM-generated phishing emails now achieve a 54% click-through rate compared to just 12% for human-written attacks — a 4.5x increase in effectiveness. One in every 323 emails hitting small business inboxes is malicious. Meanwhile, 72% of workers say phishing attempts feel more convincing than a year ago, precisely because AI has stripped out the spelling errors and odd formatting employees were trained to spot. The entry points are predictable: 32% of 2025 ransomware incidents started with an exploited software vulnerability, and 23% with compromised credentials — both entirely preventable with the right controls.

For your business, the financial stakes are real. VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report found that 40% of SMBs say a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less would shut them down entirely — and the average breach costs far more once downtime and recovery are factored in. The good news? CISA's guidance for small businesses confirms that foundational controls — MFA on every account, consistent patch management, employee phishing training, and tested backups — close the door on the vast majority of attacks before they start.

You don't have to navigate this alone. This month, we're sharing the tools and strategies that Pacific Northwest businesses are using right now to stay protected — read on for the practical steps inside this issue.
