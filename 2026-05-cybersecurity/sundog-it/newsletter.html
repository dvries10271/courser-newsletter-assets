Why Cybersecurity Is Now Every SMB's #1 Business Risk

Cybercriminals aren't just targeting large enterprises anymore — and the numbers prove it. If your business has fewer than 200 employees, you're squarely in the crosshairs of today's most active threat actors. Understanding what's changed in 2026 could mean the difference between a close call and a catastrophic loss.

The threat landscape has shifted decisively. According to VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report, for the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken economic pressures like inflation and recession as the number-one concern for small and mid-sized businesses. That's not a headline — it's a warning.

Here's what the data tells us heading into mid-2026. Small businesses are targets of 43% of all cyberattacks despite representing only 30% of the business landscape. AI-powered tools have dramatically lowered the barrier for attackers, enabling them to launch sophisticated phishing campaigns, deepfake voice scams, and business email compromise at scale. In fact, the World Economic Forum's Global Cybersecurity Outlook 2026 found that 73% of respondents reported someone in their network was personally affected by cyber-enabled fraud in 2025 — dwarfing direct ransomware impacts. Perhaps most alarming: 40% of SMBs say an attack costing $100,000 or less could shut them down permanently.

The good news is that most breaches are preventable with the right fundamentals. CISA's Cyber Guidance for Small Businesses consistently points to four essentials: enforcing multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all accounts, training employees to recognize phishing, maintaining tested data backups, and patching systems promptly. Research shows employees who receive regular simulation-based training are 7x less likely to fall for phishing — a low-cost, high-impact defense.

For your Illinois business, the question isn't whether a threat will come — it's whether your defenses are ready when it does. A proactive managed security partner can close the gaps that attackers count on finding. Elsewhere in this issue, you'll find practical tools and next steps to help you act now.

CISA & FBI Warn SMBs: Akira Ransomware Is Targeting You

A May 2026 joint advisory from CISA, the FBI, and federal partners details updated tactics used by the Akira ransomware group, which primarily targets small and medium-sized businesses across healthcare, manufacturing, finance, and IT sectors.

Agencies urge SMBs to act now: back up critical data regularly, enforce multi-factor authentication on all accounts, and prioritize patching known exploited vulnerabilities — three steps that meaningfully reduce your exposure without requiring an enterprise-level budget.

CYBER THREAT UPDATE

Ransomware Groups Now Steal Data Before Encrypting It

A growing number of ransomware attacks now follow a "double extortion" approach — thieves quietly copy your data before locking your systems, then threaten to publish it publicly if you don't pay. CISA continues to flag actively exploited vulnerabilities used as entry points, urging all organizations to prioritize patching and maintain tested backups.
