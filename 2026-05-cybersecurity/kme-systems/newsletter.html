Why Cybercriminals Are Targeting Your Business Right Now

Cybercriminals aren't just targeting big corporations anymore — they're going after businesses exactly like yours. If you think your size makes you invisible to attackers, the data tells a very different story.

In 2026, the threat landscape for small and mid-sized businesses has shifted dramatically. AI-powered tools have made it cheaper and faster than ever for attackers to launch sophisticated campaigns at scale. According to the VikingCloud 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report, cybersecurity has overtaken inflation and recession as the #1 concern for SMBs — and for good reason. For the first time, 3 in 4 SMB owners rank cyberattacks as their top operational threat.

The numbers behind the headlines are stark. The Verizon 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report found that ransomware was present in 88% of SMB breaches — compared to just 39% for large enterprises. Meanwhile, AI-generated phishing emails are achieving click-through rates of 54%, versus just 12% for human-written attacks. And 46% of SMBs say they encountered AI-generated phishing in the past 12 months. Ransomware has also evolved: attackers now steal your data before encrypting it, threatening to publish it publicly — a tactic known as double extortion — so even solid backups don't eliminate all the leverage. Perhaps most sobering: CISA's guidance for small businesses notes that the average downtime following a ransomware attack stretches to 24 days.

The good news? The most effective defenses aren't complex or out of reach for a lean team. Enforcing multi-factor authentication (MFA) across all accounts, running regular employee phishing simulations, keeping systems patched, and maintaining tested offline backups are proven to dramatically reduce your exposure. CISA calls MFA "the most important step an organization can make." Employees with consistent simulation-based training are 7x less likely to fall for phishing.
