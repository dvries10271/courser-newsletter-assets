Why Cybercriminals Love Targeting Small Businesses in 2026

Cyberattacks aren't just a big-business problem — and the numbers in 2026 make that impossible to ignore. If you run a small or mid-sized business, you're now squarely in the crosshairs, and the consequences of a breach can be business-ending.

For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken inflation and economic pressure as the top concern for SMB owners, according to VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report. What's driving that fear? A threat landscape that's evolving faster than most small teams can manage. AI-powered tools are letting attackers scan thousands of business networks simultaneously, launch hyper-personalized phishing campaigns, and automate attacks that once required serious technical skill. The result: businesses with fewer than 100 employees now receive 350% more threats than larger companies — yet most are still defending themselves with limited tools and even less time.

The stakes couldn't be higher. VikingCloud found that 40% of SMBs say a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less could shut them down for good. Phishing and credential theft remain the most common entry points, with 80% of hacking incidents tied to compromised passwords. Meanwhile, ransomware demands have averaged $2.73 million in recent cases. And yet, CISA's guidance for small businesses points out that the most impactful defenses — multi-factor authentication (MFA), regular patching, tested incident response plans, and employee training — remain widely underdeployed. Only 20% of SMBs have implemented MFA, and nearly half spend less than $1,500 per month on security overall.

For your team, the gap between "some tools in place" and "actually protected" is where the real risk lives. Attackers know that SMBs often have valuable data but weaker defenses — and they exploit that gap every day. The good news: you don't have to face this alone, and you don't need an enterprise budget to build real resilience.

This month's newsletter is packed with practical guidance to help you close those gaps — from MFA and patching basics to what a real incident response plan looks like. Want to see where your business stands right now? We're here to help.
