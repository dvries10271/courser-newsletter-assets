Your Business Is a Target — Here's What to Do About It

You know that feeling in the pit of your stomach when you realize you may have clicked something you shouldn't have? That moment of dread is something attackers are counting on — and in 2026, they're better at triggering it than ever before.

Here's the hard truth: small and mid-sized businesses are the #1 target. According to the SonicWall 2026 Cyber Protect Report, SMBs bore a disproportionate ransomware burden — 88% of their breaches involved ransomware in 2025, more than double the rate seen at large enterprises. And the average breach goes undetected for 181 days. That's six months of an attacker quietly living inside your systems.

The financial stakes are just as sobering. A single SMB breach can exceed $4.91 million when downtime and recovery are factored in. Meanwhile, VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report found that 40% of SMBs say a cyberattack costing just $100,000 or less would shut them down entirely. For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken economic pressure as the top concern for small business owners. AI-powered phishing attacks have seen a 4.5x increase in effectiveness — and attackers are no longer targeting one business at a time. They're hitting thousands simultaneously.

But here's the thing: SonicWall's own security experts note that "the vast majority of attacks are basic fundamentals that continue to be missed." This isn't about exotic threats. It's about the basics — and CISA's Cyber Essentials guidance agrees. Their top priorities for SMBs right now: enforce multi-factor authentication (MFA) on every account, automate patch management, maintain tested backups, and train your team to spot phishing before it lands.

If you're running your IT reactively — or relying on one person to hold it all together — you're already behind. The good news? You don't have to go it alone. FBI: Cybercrime Losses Hit $20B — BEC Is the Biggest SMB Threat

The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report — its first to log over 1 million complaints in a single year — puts total cybercrime losses past $20 billion, a 26% jump over 2024. Business email compromise (BEC) alone drove $3 billion in losses, making it the most financially destructive enterprise-level threat in the U.S.

Here's the gut-punch for SMBs: AI is now supercharging attacks. The report flagged over 22,000 AI-related complaints — criminals using deepfakes, voice cloning, and AI-generated phishing to hit businesses faster and smarter than ever.
