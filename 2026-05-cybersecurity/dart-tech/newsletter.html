Why Cybercriminals Are Targeting SMBs More Than Ever in 2026

If you think your business is too small to be a target, think again. Cybercriminals have done the math — and smaller businesses are increasingly their preferred mark. The question isn't whether you'll be tested. It's whether you'll be ready.

For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken economic pressure as the top concern for SMBs in 2026, according to VikingCloud's 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report. That alarm is well-founded: small and mid-sized businesses now absorb 43% of all cyberattacks, despite representing a fraction of the business landscape. Attackers have shifted strategy — automation and AI let them hit dozens of SMBs simultaneously, at scale, at a lower cost than targeting a single enterprise.

The threat mix your team faces right now is more sophisticated than ever. AI-powered phishing emails are increasingly indistinguishable from legitimate messages — in fact, 72% of workers say phishing attempts are more convincing than a year ago due to AI-written language, per a Sagiss/Pollfish survey of 500 U.S. workers in early 2026. Meanwhile, the FBI's IC3 reports Business Email Compromise (BEC) drained more than $3 billion from victims in 2025 alone. Ransomware isn't letting up either — 88% of attacks hit SMBs, and 40% of small businesses say an incident costing just $100,000 or less could shut them down for good. Compounding everything: only 34% of SMBs have a formal incident response plan, leaving most organizations to improvise under fire.

For your Tampa Bay business, this means a few high-ROI actions deserve your attention this month: enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) across all accounts — CISA calls it the single most important step any organization can take. Pair that with regular employee phishing simulations, tested data backups following the 3-2-1 rule, and a documented incident response plan.

Proactive beats reactive — every time. If your team doesn't have the bandwidth to manage these controls in-house, that's exactly where a trusted IT partner steps in. Let's talk about where your biggest gaps are.

FBI: Cybercrime Losses Top $20B — SMBs Are Prime Targets

The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report — released April 2026 — recorded over 1 million cybercrime complaints in a single year for the first time, with U.S. losses exceeding $20.9 billion. Phishing losses alone tripled to $215.8M, driven by AI-powered attacks and phishing-as-a-service platforms.

For SMBs, the takeaway is urgent: ransomware, business email compromise, and account takeover are rising in both frequency and cost — and smaller organizations remain the most vulnerable targets.
