Why Cyber Threats Are Now the #1 Risk for SMBs in 2026

For the first time ever, cybersecurity has overtaken inflation and economic uncertainty as the top concern for small and mid-sized businesses — and the numbers behind that shift are sobering. If your team hasn't revisited your defenses recently, May is the right time to do it.

The threat landscape has fundamentally changed. AI is no longer just a business tool — attackers are using it to automate phishing campaigns, clone executive voices, and craft convincing deepfake schemes at scale. According to the VikingCloud 2026 SMB Threat Landscape Report, 3 in 4 SMBs now say a cyber incident is the single event most likely to negatively impact their business this year. In that same report, 40% of SMBs admitted a cyberattack costing $100,000 or less would be enough to shut them down.

The attack mix targeting businesses like yours right now includes AI-enhanced phishing, business email compromise (BEC), ransomware with double-extortion tactics, and supply chain infiltration. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center reported over $2.7 billion in losses from BEC alone in 2024 — and that's just one vector. Meanwhile, research from Sagiss found that 72% of workers say phishing attempts are now more convincing than a year ago thanks to AI-written language. Human error remains the soft underbelly: 68% of phishing breaches trace back to a single untrained employee.

What can you do this month? CISA recommends three non-negotiable fundamentals: enforce multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all accounts, maintain tested backups, and run regular employee security awareness training. These aren't complex — but they close the gaps attackers exploit most often. Only 32% of SMBs currently invest in security awareness training, according to VikingCloud, leaving a wide-open door.

CISA and the FBI updated their joint advisory on Akira ransomware, which primarily targets small and medium-sized businesses across healthcare, IT, financial services, and manufacturing.
