Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Practice — Here's What to Do

Your team is likely using AI tools you've never approved — and they may not even realize it's a problem.

Shadow AI refers to the use of unapproved artificial intelligence tools at work: free chatbots, browser-based writing assistants, AI-powered plugins, and more. Employees turn to these tools to move faster and meet deadlines. That's understandable. But when patient records, financial data, or confidential business information enters an unvetted AI platform, your organization loses control of where that data goes — and who can access it.

The numbers tell a sobering story. According to IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report, shadow AI was involved in 20% of breaches studied and added roughly $670,000 to the average incident cost. A recent PagerDuty survey found that 66% of office professionals admitted to using AI tools for work even when they believed those tools weren't permitted — and at smaller companies, that figure still reached 60%. Meanwhile, IBM found that 63% of organizations had no AI governance policy in place at all. For healthcare practices, where HIPAA compliance is non-negotiable, this gap is especially dangerous: only 23% of healthcare employees are aware of the HIPAA implications of the AI tools they're already using.

Regulators are paying attention. NIST is preparing a new AI cybersecurity framework profile expected this summer, and CISA has already issued multinational guidance on AI risk. Cyber insurers are following suit — many now condition coverage on documented AI security controls.

The answer isn't a blanket ban. Restricting AI without providing alternatives pushes usage further underground. Instead, your practice needs a written AI Use Policy that identifies approved tools, defines what data can and cannot be entered into AI platforms, and requires human review of AI-assisted work.
