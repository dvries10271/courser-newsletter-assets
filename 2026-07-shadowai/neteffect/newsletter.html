Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Here's What to Do

Your employees are using AI right now — and there's a good chance your IT team doesn't know about it. That's the uncomfortable reality of "shadow AI," and for Las Vegas SMBs, it's quickly becoming one of the most pressing cybersecurity risks of 2026.

Shadow AI isn't a sci-fi concept. It's your sales rep pasting a client list into a free chatbot to draft emails faster, or your office manager uploading a financial report to an AI summarizer without a second thought. These tools are easy to access, instantly useful, and almost entirely outside your security perimeter. As Barracuda Networks explains, much of today's AI adoption is happening outside approved tools, processes, and oversight — and for SMBs, the risks concentrate fast.

The numbers paint a sobering picture. According to PagerDuty research cited by TechRadar, two-thirds (66%) of office professionals have used AI tools for work despite believing those tools were not permitted — and more than a third admitted to entering customer information into public AI systems. Meanwhile, ISACA's 2026 AI Pulse Poll found that while 90% of organizations report employees using AI tools, only 38% have a formal, comprehensive AI policy and one in four have no policy at all. For regulated industries like healthcare, legal, and financial services, the stakes are even higher — every unvetted AI interaction involving patient or client data is a potential HIPAA or compliance violation.

The good news? You don't need to ban AI to fix this. The most effective response is a short, clear AI Use Policy that defines what data must never enter a public AI tool, a list of approved alternatives, and basic employee training. Banning AI outright often pushes usage further underground — governed enablement is the smarter play.
