Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Here's the Risk

Your employees are already using AI tools you didn't approve — and most of them don't think twice about it. That's the reality of shadow AI, and it's one of the fastest-growing cybersecurity threats facing small and mid-sized businesses today.

Shadow AI refers to the use of AI tools — chatbots, writing assistants, code helpers, summarizers — without IT department knowledge or approval. It's not malicious intent driving this behavior. Your team members are simply trying to work faster. But the consequences can be serious. According to a June 2026 PagerDuty survey reported by TechRadar, two-thirds (66%) of office professionals have used AI tools for work even while believing company policy didn't permit it. At smaller organizations, that figure still held at 60%.

The data exposure embedded in these habits is alarming. That same study found that more than a third of employees have entered customer information into public AI systems, and 31% acknowledged uploading financial data or confidential business documents. Every one of those prompts is data leaving your environment — potentially stored, retained, or used for model training on third-party servers you have no visibility into. As Barracuda Networks noted in their June 2026 SMB security guidance, untracked AI use increases data exposure, compliance risk, and loss of visibility all at once. For businesses in regulated industries like healthcare or financial services, that can mean HIPAA or PCI violations you never saw coming.

The numbers behind the risk are stark. IBM data cited by security researchers shows shadow AI costs organizations $670,000 more per breach than those with low or no unauthorized AI usage. Yet according to ISACA's 2026 AI Pulse Poll of 3,400 digital trust professionals, 90% of organizations say employees use AI tools — while only 38% have a comprehensive AI policy, and one in four have no policy at all.

The answer isn't to ban AI outright. Restrictions alone often drive usage further underground. The right move is a formal AI Use Policy paired with approved, governed alternatives your team will actually choose to use. That means knowing what tools are in play, defining what data can never enter an AI prompt, and training your staff on the difference between helpful and harmful AI habits.
