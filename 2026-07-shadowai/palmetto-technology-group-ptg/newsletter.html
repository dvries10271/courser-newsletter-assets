Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Are You Ready?

Your employees aren't waiting for IT to hand them the future. They're already using AI tools — today, on your network, with your data. And most of them aren't asking permission first.

Shadow AI refers to the use of unauthorized or unapproved artificial intelligence tools in the workplace — think employees pasting customer records, financial data, or internal plans into public tools like ChatGPT without realizing where that information goes or who can access it. It's the AI equivalent of shadow IT, but with higher stakes: these systems don't just store data, they process and potentially retain it.

The numbers are striking. New research from PagerDuty published this month found that two-thirds (66%) of office professionals have used AI tools for work despite believing those tools were not permitted by company policy. At smaller companies, the figure still reaches 60%. Meanwhile, Cyberhaven's 2026 AI Data Report found that 77% of employees have pasted company data directly into AI or large language model services — including customer records, HR documents, and financial data. Perhaps most alarming, IBM's 2025 breach data shows shadow AI costs organizations an average of $670,000 more per breach and takes 10 additional days to contain. Yet only 38% of organizations have a formal, comprehensive AI policy in place, according to Kiteworks' 2026 Forecast.

For SMBs in South Carolina and the Southeast, this isn't a theoretical risk — it's an active exposure gap. If a team member shares a client proposal or HR record with an unvetted AI tool, your business could face data loss, compliance violations, or a breach that's far costlier to recover from than it would have been to prevent.

The good news? You don't have to choose between blocking AI and enabling productivity. A clear AI Use Policy, paired with approved Microsoft 365 Copilot tools and proper governance, keeps your team efficient and your data protected. PTG can help you build that foundation.
