Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Here's What to Do

Your employees are already using AI tools at work — and most of them aren't asking for permission first. That gap between what IT knows and what's actually running on your network has a name: shadow AI. And in 2026, it's one of the fastest-growing threats facing small and mid-sized businesses.

Shadow AI refers to the use of unauthorized, consumer-grade AI tools — chatbots, writing assistants, transcription apps, and more — outside the visibility of your IT team. Unlike traditional shadow IT, shadow AI doesn't show up on a network device list and doesn't require malware to exfiltrate data. That makes it uniquely dangerous: the exposure happens quietly, one copy-and-paste at a time.

The scale of the problem is striking. New research from PagerDuty found that 66% of office professionals have used AI tools for work despite believing those tools were not permitted — and at smaller companies, that figure is still 60%. Meanwhile, Mimecast's State of Human Risk 2026 report found that 80% of organizations worry about sensitive data leaking through generative AI tools, yet 60% still have no specific strategy to address it. The risks are real: employees may unknowingly paste customer records, financial data, or proprietary plans into public AI platforms whose privacy policies allow third-party data use.

For your business, this isn't just a technical headache — it's a compliance and liability issue. Cyber insurance carriers are increasingly conditioning coverage on the adoption of AI-specific security controls. Simply banning AI tools rarely works either; restriction alone tends to push usage further out of sight. The smarter play is a clear, enforceable AI Use Policy paired with approved, secure alternatives your team will actually adopt.
