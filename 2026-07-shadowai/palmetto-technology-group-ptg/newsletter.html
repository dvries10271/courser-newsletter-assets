Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Here's What to Do

Your employees are already using AI tools you didn't approve — and most of them don't realize the risk they're creating. This is the reality of "Shadow AI," and for small and mid-sized businesses, the exposure is growing faster than most IT teams can track.

Shadow AI isn't just rogue software. As The Hacker News explains, unlike traditional shadow IT, these tools actively process, generate, and potentially retain sensitive data — operating entirely outside the visibility of your security team. Think about it: when an employee pastes a client contract, financial record, or internal strategy document into a public AI tool, that data may leave your control permanently.

The numbers make this urgent. According to Help Net Security's May 2026 report, 7 in 10 employees use AI tools at least a few times a week, and 80% expect their usage to increase over the next year — yet many employers have provided no formal training or approved alternatives. A separate survey found that 44% of U.S. workers say their employer has no clear AI policy, or aren't sure if one even exists. That gap is exactly where Shadow AI thrives. The financial stakes are steep: IBM's 2025 data shows Shadow AI adds $670,000 more per breach and takes 10 additional days to contain compared to standard incidents.

The good news? Banning AI outright isn't the answer — it typically drives usage further underground. What actually works is a combination of visibility, clear policy, and IT-approved alternatives that give your team safe tools to accomplish their goals.

If your business doesn't yet have a formal AI Use Policy, now is the time to build one.
