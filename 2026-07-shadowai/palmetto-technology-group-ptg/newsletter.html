Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Are You Ready?

Your employees are already using AI tools you didn't approve. The question isn't *if* shadow AI has entered your business — it's how much data has already left with it.

Shadow AI refers to the use of unapproved AI tools — think personal ChatGPT accounts, browser-based AI assistants, or free generative tools — outside the visibility of your IT team. It's the natural evolution of shadow IT, but with much higher stakes. Unlike a rogue app, shadow AI actively processes and potentially retains your sensitive data, creating blind spots your security controls simply weren't built for.

The numbers tell a sobering story. According to research cited by Barracuda Networks, employees are pasting customer details, invoices, HR records, and internal plans into public AI tools without a second thought. A Microsoft and LinkedIn Work Trend Index report found that 78% of employees use their own AI tools at work — especially at SMBs — and 52% are reluctant to admit it. Meanwhile, IBM data shows that shadow AI costs organizations an average of **$670,000 more per breach** and takes 10 additional days to contain. And according to the ESET SMB Cyber Readiness Index 2026, many SMBs are still underestimating the risk entirely.

Here's what this means for your business: banning AI outright won't work — it just pushes usage further underground. What you need is visibility, governance, and an AI Use Policy that sets clear expectations for your team. That means identifying approved tools, classifying what data can and can't be shared with AI systems, and training employees on safe usage. Cyber insurers are already asking whether you have these controls in place — and "we didn't know" is no longer an acceptable answer.
