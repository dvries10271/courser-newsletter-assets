Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business—Here's What to Do

Your employees are using AI tools right now—and most of them haven't told IT. That's the reality of shadow AI, and for SMBs across the Southeast, it's quickly becoming one of the most urgent cybersecurity risks of 2026.

Shadow AI isn't just a buzzword. It's what happens when staff members turn to free or personal AI tools—think public chatbots, browser-based writing assistants, or AI transcription services—to get work done faster, without going through approved channels. The impulse is understandable. According to Help Net Security, seven in ten employees now use AI tools at least a few times a week, and 80% expect their AI use to grow over the next year. Many are doing this with little to no guidance from their employers.

The problem isn't AI itself—it's AI outside your control. When your team pastes a client contract, financial record, or internal strategy into an unapproved tool, that data can leave your environment entirely. Barracuda Networks notes that simply banning AI use often drives the behavior further underground—visibility has to come first. And the financial stakes are real: according to research cited by UnderDefense, shadow AI costs organizations an average of $670,000 more per breach and takes 10 additional days to contain. Meanwhile, cyber insurance carriers are beginning to require documented AI governance controls—and Kiteworks warns that organizations without them may face coverage gaps or higher premiums.

For your business, this means the window to get ahead of shadow AI is narrowing fast. The good news: you don't need to prohibit AI—you need to govern it. A clear AI Use Policy, paired with approved tools and basic employee training, closes the most dangerous gaps without slowing your team down.
