Shadow AI Is Already Inside Your Business — Here's What to Do

Your employees are using AI tools right now — and there's a good chance your IT team has no idea which ones.

That's the reality of shadow AI: unauthorized AI tools adopted by staff without IT approval, operating entirely outside your security perimeter. It's the AI-era evolution of shadow IT, but with far higher stakes. Every prompt submitted to an unvetted tool is potentially sensitive data — client records, financial documents, internal strategies — flowing to third-party servers with unknown retention and security policies.

The numbers are hard to ignore. According to a June 2026 PagerDuty study reported by TechRadar, 66% of office professionals have used AI tools for work despite believing those tools were not permitted — and more than a third admitted to entering customer information into public AI systems. Meanwhile, ISACA's 2026 AI Pulse Poll of 3,400 digital trust professionals found that while 90% of organizations report employees using AI tools, only 38% have a formal, comprehensive AI policy — and 1 in 4 have no policy at all. The financial exposure is equally stark: IBM data cited by security researchers shows shadow AI costs organizations an average of $670,000 more per breach than those with low or no unauthorized AI usage.

For South Florida firms in legal, healthcare, and financial services, this isn't just a productivity concern — it's a compliance crisis. HIPAA, financial regulations, and client confidentiality obligations don't make exceptions for well-intentioned employees looking to work faster. As Barracuda Networks notes, simply banning AI often drives usage further out of sight. The smarter play is visibility first, governance second: know what tools your team is using, establish an AI Use Policy that defines approved tools and prohibited data types, and offer sanctioned alternatives that meet your team's real workflow needs.
