Microsoft Copilot Cowork: Your AI That Actually Does the Work

What if you could hand off a complex project task, step away from your desk, and come back to find it done? That is no longer a far-off idea — it is what Microsoft Copilot Cowork is built to deliver, and it is available right now.

Microsoft made Copilot Cowork generally available worldwide on June 16, 2026, capping a three-month Frontier preview program. Unlike traditional AI chat tools that suggest and summarize, Cowork is designed to take action. It handles long-running, multi-tool workflows across Microsoft 365 apps, files, and your organization's data — executing tasks rather than just recommending them. Think of it as the difference between an assistant who gives you advice and one who actually completes the work.

Here is what makes Cowork a meaningful step forward for your team. Simply describe the outcome you want, and Cowork creates a plan, reasons across your tools and files, and carries the work forward — with visible progress and checkpoints so you stay in control. Early users have reported workflows ranging from orchestrating inboxes and conducting research to generating structured documents and preparing for executive reviews. Cowork also runs in the cloud, so tasks keep progressing even after you close your laptop. And with a new mobile app for iOS and Android, you can delegate work the moment you think of it — on your commute or between meetings — and return to a finished result.

For small and medium-sized businesses in Eastern Wisconsin, the timing is significant. As of July 1, 2026, Microsoft bundled Copilot directly into its Business Standard and Business Premium plans as permanent SKUs — making AI-powered productivity a built-in standard rather than an optional add-on. That means your team may already have access to these capabilities today.
