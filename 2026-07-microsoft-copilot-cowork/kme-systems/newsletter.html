Microsoft Copilot Cowork Is Here — and It Changes Everything

What if your AI didn't just answer questions — but actually finished the work? That's the shift Microsoft made official this summer, and it has real implications for every business running Microsoft 365.

On June 16, 2026, Microsoft launched Copilot Cowork into general availability worldwide, bringing its most ambitious agentic AI capability out of preview and into everyday business use. Unlike standard Copilot Chat — which helps you draft emails or summarize documents — Cowork is designed to execute long-running, multi-step tasks across Outlook, Teams, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and SharePoint without you switching between apps. You define the goal. Cowork sees it through to a finished deliverable.

This is a meaningful evolution. According to Microsoft's June 2026 Copilot update, Cowork is powered by Work IQ, which grounds every task in your organization's actual data and systems — so the output reflects real business context, not generic AI responses. It runs in a secure, cloud-hosted environment, meaning tasks continue even when your laptop is off. And it's built on a multi-model foundation, with both OpenAI GPT 5.5 and Anthropic's Claude available depending on the type of work at hand.

For your team, the practical upside is significant: complex workflows that once took hours of coordination can now be delegated to an AI that plans, acts, and delivers. But there are also governance considerations worth knowing. Cowork is off by default — an admin must enable it — and it uses usage-based billing through Copilot Credits, with a Cost Management Dashboard to keep spend in check.
