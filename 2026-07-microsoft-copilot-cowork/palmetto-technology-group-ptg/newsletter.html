Microsoft Copilot Cowork: Your AI Teammate Is Now Open for Business

What if you could delegate an entire multi-step project to an AI—and come back to a finished result, not just a rough draft? That's exactly what Microsoft just made possible for businesses like yours.

On June 16, 2026, Microsoft made Copilot Cowork generally available worldwide for Microsoft 365 Copilot customers. What started as a limited preview quickly became the fastest-growing feature in the Frontier program's history—and it's not hard to see why. This isn't another chatbot. Copilot Cowork represents a fundamental shift from "AI that answers questions" to "AI that completes work."

Here's what Cowork actually does inside your Microsoft 365 environment: you describe what you need, and it takes action. According to Microsoft Learn, Cowork sends emails through Outlook, schedules meetings, builds Word documents, creates Excel spreadsheets, and posts in Teams—all on your behalf, with you approving each action before it happens. For longer, cross-app tasks, it can pull data from SharePoint, draft summaries in Word, format reports in Excel, and queue results for review in Teams—end-to-end, without you switching between apps. Microsoft reports that Cowork "has among the highest user satisfaction of any Copilot or agent experience we have shipped." Enterprise controls matter too: IT admins can set spending limits, manage billing through Copilot Credits, and monitor agent activity through Microsoft Sentinel for full governance and visibility.

For your team, this means fewer hours lost to repetitive coordination work and more time focused on the decisions that actually move your business forward. Whether you're in professional services, construction, or manufacturing, delegating routine workflows to Cowork can reduce bottlenecks and keep projects on track.
